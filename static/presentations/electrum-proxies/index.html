Electrum · CVE-2026-117601 · exploited in the wild

Fake Electrum servers are hijacking fee bumps

Dozens of new "Electrum servers" quietly proxy real ones. At least 18 of them forge your just-broadcast transaction so that, if you bump its fee, your wallet signs a payment to the attacker.

Spotted by b10c (2026-11-23) · confirmed by b10c and Andrew Toth (2026-11-24) · bnoc.xyz thread · advisory

Background

What an Electrum client trusts its server with

From Electrum's own FAQ: confirmed transactions are verified with SPV; "the server is trusted about unconfirmed transactions" and "can lie by omission."

The setup

A fleet of proxies in front of real servers

37+ domains

Most registered Nov 15–19; a batch from Dec 2025–Jan 2026 got certs the same week. Self-signed, 10-year TLS certs.

Cheap VPS hosting

Mostly Vultr, plus MHost, PsychoVPS, DigitalOcean and others, across NL, AU, SG, JP, GB, PL and US.

Seen from the real side

Emzy: 52 connections from 19 proxies. Seth: 41 connections from 20 of the IPs.

The attack

Swallow, forge, and wait for a fee bump

The forged tx2 can't be mined, since its signatures are bad. The theft only works if the user re-signs it with a fee bump. One reporter did, and lost funds.

Ground address

Same bc1p type, same last 4 characters. Anyone who checks only the ends of an address is fooled.

Same amount

The recipient value is untouched, so the transaction looks right at a glance.

Primed to bump

RBF is switched on and the fee shaved. In the advisory's loss case, the forged tx had zero fee.

Timeline and fix

Disclosed in October, exploited in November

  • The fix (4.8.3, commit 90fe308): before adding an unconfirmed tx to history, the wallet verifies the signatures on inputs that spend its own coins. A forged tx2 is rejected.
  • Still trusted: incoming unconfirmed payments. Checking those would need a script interpreter and every unconfirmed parent.

What to do

Protect yourself, then discuss

Upgrade

Electrum 4.8.3 or later. Every version seen connected through the proxies was vulnerable.

Pick your server

Run your own, or pin a trusted one with auto-connect off. Clients on servers they trust weren't affected.

Read before you sign

Check the full recipient address and amount on every signature, fee bumps included, ideally on a hardware wallet screen.

  • Most proxy domains were registered about three weeks after the public advisory. Was disclosure the starting gun? Should fixes like this come with forced updates?
  • Servers are found by gossip, so anyone can advertise one. How the proxies became wallets' main server is still unknown. How should light clients choose whom to trust?
  • Ground suffixes beat a "check the last few characters" habit. What should wallets show instead?
  • Server operators saw the proxies as idle clients. Could public servers detect and block relaying?
